The dataset contains 125,973 training records and 22,544 testing records, with 42 columns consisting of 41 input features and one target label. Abstract:
The growing complexity and advancement of the modern cyberattacks have proven the insecurity of the old-fashioned cybersecurity tools, in the form of firewall and signature-based intrusion detecting tools. Evolution of advanced protracted threats, phishing, ransomware and constantly-changing malware has made a sort of a defensive strategy rather useless. Artificial Intelligence (AI), specifically, the use of Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL) in the real-time detection, classification, and reduction of cyber threats is more dynamic and reactive in this instance. The article discusses the application of AI to cybersecurity and machine learning techniques of intrusion detection, anomaly detection, phishing detection and malware classification in particular. It incorporates the in-depth analysis of the existing methods and provides an AI-based cybersecurity framework that would assist in enhancing the process of threats detection. The experimental evaluations are done using the benchmark datasets such as NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017 to compare the performance of different approaches. Other critical issues that are discussed in the study are adversarial attacks on machine learning models, lack of explainability, and data imbalance issues. Lastly, it highlights research directions in the future with a focus on Explainable AI and Federated Learning, and quantum-enabled security systems in enhancing next-generation cybersecurity infrastructures.
